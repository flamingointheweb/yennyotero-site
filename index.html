bachata-connect · what I built

The wristband that identifies you at the event.

I built a bespoke NFC check-in for Bachata Week Cádiz, where 400 dancers from 45+ countries arrive every year. Tapping the wristband opens a live pass that staff can check at a glance, so hundreds of people get in without anyone reviewing a bracelet or a tap.

The pass can’t be copied. Its colour, ring angle, glow and pattern are all calculated from the server clock, so every real pass shows exactly the same frame at the same moment. A screenshot or recording falls out of step within a second.

  • ✓screenshots and recordings fall out of step in a second
  • ✓344 guests checked in over two afternoons

One codebase, three artist brands.

Artists sell their own courses on the platform, each on a site in their own brand: colours, fonts, corners and language, set in one file per artist. A new artist is a data change, not a rebuild. Three brands so far, all running on the same code.

From a lesson video to a paid online course.

An artist gave me his lesson videos. I took the transcripts, translated them and built the course content from them. Then I built a player for how dancers actually learn, and a way for students to get personal feedback from the artist, which they can pay for. Claude translates that feedback on demand: 325 reviews for under a euro.

A festival app, live in three weeks.

Pulse is a three-day bachata festival in London. Three weeks before it opened, I took on its app: the wristband is the entry pass, tapping it opens your profile, and tapping someone else’s saves them as someone you met. Around it sit the schedule, the teachers and a ten-second way to tell the organisers how it’s going. Help comes first on the Info page. The report form lets you stay anonymous, and a team member can fill it in with you. A reported class post is hidden at once and sent to the organiser’s phone. I merged about 190 small pull requests in the ten days before and during the festival, several of them on opening day.

About

I’m a designer who builds. I started as a developer at Opera Software, where I was employee 63 and worked on browsers for the Nintendo Wii and DS. I moved into design, then spent a decade in financial services leading customer experience. AI made shipping fast, so I went back to building.

Bachata Connect, the platform I built and run, is used at live festivals. I work in small pull requests with tests and CI, and the design system is public.

I care most about products where behaviour matters more than features: money, health and community. I have just finished an Executive MBA at Warwick Business School on why people avoid dealing with their money. I speak Spanish, English, Italian and Norwegian.
