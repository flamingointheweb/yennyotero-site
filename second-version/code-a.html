01

A festival app, live in three weeks.

Pulse is a three-day bachata festival in London. Three weeks before it opened, I took on its app: the wristband is the entry pass, tapping it opens your profile, and tapping someone else’s saves them as someone you met. Around it sit the schedule, the teachers, safety information and a ten-second way to tell the organisers how it’s going. I merged about 190 small pull requests in the ten days before and during the festival, several of them on opening day.

03

Designing what an AI is allowed to say.

An assistant that answers participants’ questions about next year’s event, but only from facts it is given, with every source checked by the server. Anything it can’t verify becomes “not sure”, and anything sensitive goes to a person. I built a test bench for it: 29 questions, scored for correct, grounded, right hand-off and tone. Every failure in the tests turned into a design fix, taking it from 26 to 29 of 29 correct, at $0.13 a run. It stays switched off for participants until the programme it answers about is complete. Claude also translates expert video feedback from Spanish on demand: 325 reviews for under a euro.

04

Safety and privacy as product work.

At Pulse, help comes first on the Info page: emergency, need help now, witnessing something, report. I first built the report flow with its own separate database, then switched every entry point to the safety team’s own form when they asked, within a day. Class posts can be reported by anyone: the post is hidden at once, the organiser’s phone gets a push, and they can restore it, remove it or pause the poster. Before the festival I audited my own database permissions, found that profile fields were readable by any signed-in account, and fixed it with column-level grants, checked as a real user, the same day.

A design system that can’t ship unreadable text.

contrast.test.ts
// every text colour, on every ground, in every palette
describe.each(palettes)('palette "%s"', (name, t) => {
  for (const role of TEXT_ROLES) {
    it(`${role} reads everywhere (≥ 4.5:1)`, () => {
      const worst = Math.min(
        ...GROUNDS.map((bg) => contrastRatio(t[role], t[bg]))
      )
      expect(worst).toBeGreaterThanOrEqual(4.5)
    })
  }
})

Fifteen colour roles, three brands, one component library. Every pull request checks 86 colour pairs, each text colour on each background it sits on. Our own brand gold was 2.6:1 as link text; it’s fixed, and the test stops it coming back.

About

I am from Colombia, spent sixteen years in Norway and now live in London. My first job was as a developer at Opera Software, where I was employee 69 and worked on browsers for the Nintendo Wii and DS and for Motorola phones.

On the DSi I was the interaction designer: smooth scrolling, transitions that felt like a game, and pages you could browse with one hand. I have cared about how an interface feels in the hand ever since.

I moved from code into design, then into customer experience, and spent the last decade in financial services. Most of my work has been with people at difficult moments: borrowers in trouble, patients waiting for a diagnosis, older people adjusting to a hearing aid. That is where behaviour matters more than features.

I’ve led teams at two banks. I went back to building because AI has made shipping fast, and the hard part now is deciding what’s worth shipping. I have just finished an Executive MBA at Warwick Business School, where my research looked at why people avoid dealing with their money. I speak Spanish, English, Italian and Norwegian.
