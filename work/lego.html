1. Building the team and the standards

I have built a design function from nothing, and made quality normal.

At TSB I joined as the first in-house designer in 2018, in the middle of a crisis. Over five years I hired and led a team that grew to seventeen across product, research and content. At Vanquis I led a team of fifteen.

01

Hiring and setting the bar

I built the team around the work, with design, research and content working together. There were no product managers at TSB, so my team and I led the roadmap as well as the design.

02

Systems and standards

I built the TSB design system so that teams stopped solving the same problem in different ways, and so a change could reach the whole app. It also carried the rebrand across the product and the campaign pages.

03

Testing as a habit

I made continuous testing with customers part of how teams worked, so decisions rested on evidence. That included A/B tests and working with marketing in the CMS.

04

Working with engineers

I sat with the IBM engineering team to get the app built. It taught me to write requirements an engineer can use, and to agree the standard of quality before the build, not after.

Full story: Rebuilding a banking app people could trust again.

2. Trust, clarity and accessibility

I design for people who are nervous, older or not confident.

01

Trust under pressure

I joined TSB days into a platform migration that locked customers out of their accounts. The first job was to reassure people and answer them quickly, then fix the places where they lost confidence, in small steps they could follow.

02

Inclusive design that is checked, not assumed

At Vanquis I designed how inclusive design was put into practice under FCA guidelines, so internal teams could review it and so it could be verified as usable by vulnerable customers.

03

Clear for people who are scared of breaking things

For Amplifon I tested a hearing aid companion app with older users in Italy, again and again. The settings audiologists care about became small actions, and the tone was reassuring, not clinical. It won six international awards.

04

Fun and snappy on a games device

I was the interaction designer on the Nintendo DSi browser, working with Nintendo's engineers in Tokyo. Two screens, a stylus, one-handed browsing, and transitions that felt like a game.

05

Privacy built into the design

In my own product, Bachata Connect, a person can privately remember someone they met. The other person is never told, and the app can never reveal that someone remembered you or that you were not remembered back. That rule is in the data model, not just the interface.

I am also a parent. I watch how my child moves between screens and real play, and it gives me a steady supply of ideas about what makes that feel magical and what makes it feel like a trap.

Full stories: Amplifon and Bachata Connect.

3. Shipping across devices

I own a product from the idea to what people hold in their hands.

In 2026 I designed and built a platform for my own event, Bachata Week Cádiz. It is one app with a different view for participants, door staff, teachers and admins, and nothing they need is more than two taps away on a phone in a loud room.

3 brandsone component library. A new artist page is a data change, not a rebuild
86 colour pairscontrast-tested on every pull request, so a failing colour cannot ship
344 arrivalschecked in over two afternoons with NFC wristbands

I also change my mind when the evidence says so. The first version had a noticeboard, and after testing it with people I removed it. The wristband was meant to be a social habit, but only 38% of holders tapped someone, so I let it be the moment you arrive and moved finding people again to the directory.

The design system behind it is public: github.com/flamingointheweb/design-system-showcase.

Why hire me

I bring what is hardest to hire for.

  • I have shipped on game devices. I designed the interface of the browsers on the Nintendo Wii and DSi, on consoles that families play on, working with engineers in Tokyo.
  • I have run live products on mobile and web. The TSB app and web for millions of customers, and the new app and web interface for the SAS loyalty programme, each one released and improved again and again.
  • I build teams and standards that last. Zero to 17 at TSB, with a design system and testing with customers as a habit.
  • I come from places where trust is the product. Banking taught me to design for people who are nervous, older or vulnerable, and to prove it with them, under FCA rules.
  • I still build. I design and ship in code, so I work well with engineers and I know what is possible.

What I would bring on day one is the team, the quality bar, and trust built into the product.
